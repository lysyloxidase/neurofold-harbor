# v8.0 — measured defects and a negative result about benchmark design

This report corrects claims made in the v8.0.0 release. Everything below is a
measurement, not a reinterpretation, and every number was produced by the code
in this repository.

**Short version.** v8.0.0 claims that relational structure is necessary to solve
these tasks. It is not. A policy with three hand-set weights and no training
saturates three of the five tasks. The claim rested on an experiment (A1) that
compares a full graph controller against a *crippled* graph controller, never
against a cheap one, and on a gate (A3) designed to catch exactly this, which
was specified and never run.

Four subsequent attempts to redesign the benchmark so that relational structure
*would* be necessary all failed, in a way that now looks structural rather than
accidental. That negative result is the substantive contribution here.

---

## 1. Three weights saturate three tasks

A policy vector with **3 non-zero parameters out of 2541**, written by hand in a
minute, scored through the released verifier on the hidden final-test split:

| task | reward | extended | catastrophe |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| `huntington-htt-polyq-v8` | **1.000** | **1.562** | 24.2% |
| `parkinson-alpha-synuclein-v8` | **1.000** | 1.045 | 23.4% |
| `alzheimer-tau-v8` | **1.000** | 1.086 | 11.7% |
| `alzheimer-abeta42-v8` | 0.795 | 0.795 | 19.5% |
| `als-ftd-tdp43-v8` | 0.581 | 0.581 | 31.3% |

The policy reads one observable edge feature (`ladder` membership), selects the
edge carrying it, and applies full strength. Nothing else.

HTT is the sharpest case: the frozen "challenge reference", trained for 12000
episodes with sep-CMA-ES, is **beaten outright** by the 3-weight policy on both
reward (1.562 extended) and safety (24.2% vs 34.4% catastrophe). Its upper anchor
is not a challenge.

On the public validation split the same comparison, using the porting gate's
hand-coded oracle rather than an artifact, gives 0.888 to 1.077 of the
reference's gain across the five tasks.

## 2. The reward pays for something other than what the task advertises

`instruction.md` asks the agent to "suppress pathological β-register formation".
Decomposing the reference's advantage over no-op into weighted components, on 32
public validation episodes:

| task | pathology_reduction | improvement | path_gain | damage |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `alzheimer-abeta42-v8` | 14.2% | 35.1% | 26.3% | 30.9% |
| `parkinson-alpha-synuclein-v8` | **−101.6%** | 63.9% | 82.3% | 74.3% |
| `alzheimer-tau-v8` | **1.3%** | 35.5% | 27.1% | 54.3% |
| `als-ftd-tdp43-v8` | **0.1%** | 41.3% | 39.8% | 29.9% |
| `huntington-htt-polyq-v8` | 23.6% | 18.1% | 4.2% | 45.7% |

`pathology_reduction` carries the **largest weight in the objective (0.34)** and
contributes between 0.1% and 24% of the gain. On α-synuclein it contributes
negatively: the reference is worse than doing nothing on pathology and wins
anyway.

Raw values are negative in every task under every arm — pathology *rises*. On tau
and TDP-43 the reference and no-op are indistinguishable to four decimals
(−0.0265 vs −0.0264, −0.0287 vs −0.0287).

The two energy terms are **anti-correlated with the pathology term**:

```
corr(path_gain,   pathology_reduction) = -0.733
corr(improvement, pathology_reduction) = -0.548
```

This is not a bug in the weights, it is physics: the pathological β-register is
energetically favourable, so "lower the energy" and "reduce pathology" pull in
opposite directions. The reward's energy terms (combined weight 0.26, empirically
26–82% of the reference's advantage) pay partly for driving the system *toward*
aggregation.

## 3. Root cause: pathology was not controllable at all

Under v8.0 physics, no policy reduces pathology below its initial value in more
than 3–16% of episodes — including arms that cheat by reading the ground-truth
ladder state:

| arm | Δ pathology (Aβ42) | episodes reduced |
|---|--:|--:|
| no-op | +0.679 | 6% |
| learned reference | +0.603 | 0% |
| ladder-max (cheating) | +0.612 | 0% |
| oldest-rung (cheating) | +0.736 | 3% |

Stress tests confirm this is structural, not a matter of strength or budget:

| intervention | Δ pathology |
|---|--:|
| persistent modulation, 4× budget, locking disabled | +0.548 |
| suppressing the **entire ladder** at full strength every step, no decay | +0.581 |
| best variant over a sweep of confinement, crowding, kT, relaxation rate | −0.04 vs no-op |
| shipped physics, whole-ladder suppression vs no-op | **+0.045 (worse)** |

Widening the action's reach makes it *worse*, not better.

The cause is a missing causal link. `beta_ladder()` in `neurofold8/energy.py`
decides ladder membership from geometry alone — distance, alignment, sequence
β-propensity. The agent's only lever sets `mod`, which suppresses pair *energy*.
`mod` never appears in the predicate. The only connection is Metropolis
relaxation, and it carries almost no signal.

So the reward compensated by paying for energy and damage, which is exactly what
section 2 measures.

## 4. What A1 actually tested

A1 is reported in v8.0.0 as evidence that relational structure is necessary:
+0.557 on Aβ42, 95% CI [+0.126, +0.698].

The number is correct. The interpretation is not. A1 compares a full graph
controller against the same controller with its edge embedding zeroed — a
*crippled* graph controller. It never compares against a cheap controller. Both
arms lose to three hand-set weights, so the comparison answers "does a GNN beat a
damaged GNN", not "is any of this necessary".

A3, the gate specified precisely to answer the second question — *a ≤40-parameter
controller must not reach ≥95% of full-policy gain* — appears in
`agentic/specs/ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA.md` and was never executed for v8.0.

## 5. A split-hygiene defect

`porting_gate.py` used development seeds 2000–2047, which fully contain the
public validation split 2000–2031. Every P1–P8 design decision was therefore
measured on the same 32 episodes agents are told to use for model selection.
Not final-test contamination, but the gate's evidence and the agent's selection
set were not independent. The release audit checked `train ∩ validation` and
`final ∩ *` and never checked this pair.

## 6. Four attempts to fix it, and why they failed

Work on a v9 line (unpublished, branch `v9.1-relational-composition`) tried to
make relational structure necessary. Each attempt changed the objective; none
changed the outcome.

| attempt | change | result |
|---|---|---|
| v9 | couple `mod` into the ladder predicate, so a blocked rung stops being a rung | pathology became controllable — driven below its initial value in 62–78% of episodes vs 6% for no-op. **A3 FAIL**: 3 weights reach 96.7% |
| v9 | pathology as a convex sum over critical nuclei, so *which* rung is cut matters | **A3 FAIL**: 31-parameter readout reaches 95.2% |
| v9.1 | decoys, assemblies welded through shared beads, three chains, `ladder` removed from the observation | aliasing test: relational gain **+0.011 AUC** |
| v9.1 | also delete `dist`, `contact`, `beta`, `align` — every component of the ladder predicate | local AUC only falls 0.806 → 0.738; relational gain **+0.012** |

The mechanism fix in the first row is real and worth keeping: it repaired a
genuinely broken causal chain. It simply did not make the architecture necessary.

### The aliasing measurement

88 236 (state, contact) pairs over 1056 snapshots. Ground truth is a
counterfactual measured in the simulator under common random numbers, independent
of any policy and of the reward:

```
V(s,i,j) = pathology(block (i,j), then H=12 steps) - pathology(no-op, H steps)
```

* `V == 0` **exactly** in 96.3% of pairs; `|V| ≈ 0.88` when it is not. Blocking a
  contact usually does nothing, and occasionally does a great deal.
* local probe, 11 edge features: **AUC 0.806**
* relational probe, 145 features from two rounds of aggregation: **AUC 0.817**
* selecting a contact that matters, top-1: local 36.7%, relational 42.5%

Probe machinery verified before drawing conclusions: a linear target in the local
features gives R² = 1.000, and node degree — a purely relational quantity —
gives R² = 1.000 for the relational probe against 0.126 for the local one. The
instrument separates local from relational when a difference exists.

### Why this is structural

In a coarse-grained contact-selection task, a contact's pathological status *is*
a local geometric-chemical property: the ladder predicate is a threshold
conjunction of distance, contact strength, alignment and β-propensity, and the
survivors of any deletion (notably contact age) re-encode it. Redesigning the
objective changes what a block is *worth*; it does not change *which* contact to
pick. Picking is the whole task, since the agent selects one edge per step.

Breaking this would require changing the action semantics — acting on regions or
schedules rather than contacts — which is a different benchmark, not a repair of
this one.

## 6b. Three defects in the Monte Carlo, and why they are not fixed here

Auditing the dynamics rather than the statistics found three defects in the core
sampler. All three are in the shipped v8.0 code.

**Every proposal within a step was the same displacement.** The thermal noise was
indexed by step alone, never by the proposal index:

```python
for m in range(n_metro):                                  # n_metropolis = 5
    noise = sigma_th * self._crn_thermal[self.steps]      # no dependence on m
```

An accepted move was therefore re-proposed unchanged, five times per step: a
systematic drift along one vector rather than a random walk.

**Acceptance draws were reused across steps.** `_crn_accept[(step + m) % T]` gave
480 acceptance decisions per episode from 98 numbers — each uniform deciding 4.9
moves, with neighbouring steps sharing draws in a fixed pattern.

**The re-formation penalty broke detailed balance.** The barrier was a function of
the *current* state added to ΔE regardless of direction, so A→B and B→A carried
different costs. The chain had no stationary distribution; describing its output
as thermodynamic sampling was not correct.

All three are fixed on the branch `v8.1-physics-fixes`, each with a regression
test, and common random numbers across policies are preserved and tested.

### Why the fix is not released

Correcting the sampler breaks the task. Public validation split, frozen
calibration:

| arm | v8.0 catastrophe | corrected sampler |
|---|--:|--:|
| no-op | 0.667 | 0.875 |
| oracle s=0.6 | 0.146 | 0.750 |
| oracle s=1.0 | **0.021** | 0.781 |
| blind s=1.0 | 0.500 | 0.812 |

Targeted intervention stops working. All four arms collapse into 0.75–0.88 and
become indistinguishable, at 2.2 accepted moves per step. The frozen Aβ42
reference scores **0.00076** instead of 1.0.

The finding is that **v8.0's physical parameters were implicitly fitted to the
broken sampler**. `elastic_limit`, the damage coefficients, `thermal_noise` and
the safety thresholds were all calibrated against a chain that sampled nothing
and drifted in one direction. A correct sampler exposes the calibration as
compensation for the defect.

Recovering a working task means recalibrating damage and reward from scratch,
then rerunning P1–P8 on five proteins, retraining, refreezing and reauditing,
with no guarantee that separation between targeted and blind intervention
returns. Tuning `thermal_noise` until the oracle works again would be fitting the
physics to a desired result. The branch is kept as a research record instead.

## 6c. Two smaller defects, fixed here

**A dead biological declaration.** `salt_bridge` (D23–K28) was declared in four
profiles and read by no code. It is an Aβ42-specific loop feature and had been
copied verbatim into tau, TDP-43 and α-synuclein, where those positions are
unrelated residues. Removed: an unimplemented claim that reads like a
justification is worse than no claim.

**No check that the ten copies of the simulator agree.** `neurofold8/` exists in
ten copies (five tasks × environment/tests) with no single source of truth. Any
drift between `environment/` and `tests/` would train an agent on different
dynamics than the verifier scores, and nothing tested for it. The audit now
checks it (`H10_env_tests_identical`). The copies are currently identical.

## 6d. Resolution limits worth stating plainly

At 5 residues per bead, the motifs that nucleate these aggregates occupy about
one bead:

| motif | residues | beads |
|---|---|--:|
| Aβ42 KLVFF | 15–19 | 1.0 |
| tau PHF6* VQIINK | 7–12 | 1.2 |
| tau PHF6 VQIVYK | 38–43 | 1.2 |

Steric-zipper interdigitation is a side-chain phenomenon at ångström scale and is
not representable at this coarse-graining. Bead features are averages over five
residues, so a hexapeptide motif is averaged with its neighbours. This is a
deliberate choice of resolution, but it means the mechanism being named is not
the mechanism being simulated.

Separately, `align = |u·uᵀ|` uses the absolute cosine, so **parallel and
antiparallel registers are indistinguishable** to the energy model — while the
seeding routine is named `seeded_antiparallel_pair` and the biology distinguishes
them sharply (tau filaments are parallel in-register).

## 7. What stands

* The tasks run, build, verify, and score deterministically. The Harbor
  deliverable gate passes 5/5 on all eight checks; the oracle reproduces 1.0 from
  a clean clone.
* The artifact contract holds against a 19-case red team.
* Splits are disjoint, final-test seeds are per-task and were generated at freeze.
* The TDP-43 record — heuristic P8 FAIL together with a pre-registered
  learned-control diagnostic at 8.59%, CI [5.5%, 12.1%] — remains accurate.
* Order dependence is real: a purpose-built A6 gate finds that permuting an
  identical action multiset changes the outcome in **100%** of episodes, mean
  |ΔU| = 0.0703, CI [0.0368, 0.1262].

## 8. What this benchmark should be said to measure

Not relational reasoning. What it does measure is control of a stochastic,
partially observed, hysteretic process under a finite action budget, where the
order of interventions matters and a heavy damage tail punishes averaging.

That is a real problem. It is simply not the problem the v8.0.0 README claimed.

## 9. Reusable instruments

Built during this work and applicable to any benchmark making a
"requires-architecture" claim:

* `_dev/gate_a3.py` — effective-dimensionality gate with nested masks, matched
  simulator budgets, restarts, and a hierarchical bootstrap.
* `_dev/verify_a3_masks.py` — proves an ablation is what it claims. It caught
  that a nominally 38-parameter arm had **3** parameters able to move the action.
* `_dev/gate_a6.py` — action-order dependence with a three-valued verdict.
* `_dev/gate_aliasing.py` — information-level test with a Bayes ceiling for
  local-only policies. It cost 12 minutes and answered what hours of policy
  training had been circling.
* `_dev/test_physics.py` — 12 simulator invariants. v8.0 had none below the
  statistical gates, so every physics defect had to surface as a failed gate.

The cheap information-level test should have come first. Running expensive policy
gates before measuring whether the information is even present was the main
process error in this work.
